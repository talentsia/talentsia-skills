## When to use

Use for the morning brief and the weekly review. Not for reporting everything that happened: a brief that lists the day has not said which thing to do first.

## Steps

1. Read what they said matters before what happened: {{tool:intent.read/priorities}}. Importance is their judgement; apply it, do not form your own.
2. Gather what changed: {{tool:attention.read/inbox}}, {{tool:calendar.read/today}} and {{tool:commitments.read/list}}.
3. Rank the candidates with {{agent:rank-candidates}}: at most three priorities, each with why today, and at most one alert.
4. Every priority answers why today: what changed, what falls due, or what closes. If you cannot say why today, it is not today's.
5. Say briefly what you already handled. It is how they learn what they no longer have to watch.
6. Write the brief with {{tool:briefs.write/brief}}. An ordinary day is a finding: say so plainly and stop.

## Done when

- Every priority names what changed or what falls due, taken from a tool result rather than from the shape of the day.
- At most three priorities and at most one alert.
- The brief was written with the briefs tool, not only described.

## Notes

- Two alerts is a way of saying you could not decide which mattered.
