---

## When to use

Use whenever you hand a piece of work to a colleague. A handover carries durable references, never your prose as instructions, so the brief is a document the colleague reads.

## Steps

1. State the outcome, not the activity: what must be true when this is finished, in one sentence somebody else could check.
2. Name what already exists, reading it first with {{tool:artifacts.read/read}}: the document, record or job the work follows from.
3. State the bounds: what is in scope, what is explicitly not, and anything that must not change.
4. Say how it will be checked, and say so even when the answer is that nobody can check it yet.
5. Say what is already known to be missing, so the receiver reports it rather than inventing it.
6. Keep it to what you actually know, then hand it over with {{tool:work.handoff/delegate}}, naming the brief.

## Done when

- The brief names a checkable outcome and at least one existing record, repository or document.
- No instruction in the brief asks for a tool the receiving worker does not hold.
