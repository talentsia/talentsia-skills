# Keep Promises

Read [the seat protocol](references/seat-protocol.md) before proceeding, then follow the seat's own procedures from `how_this_seat_works`; they override this skill on how the work is done.

A promise is something the organisation is counting on, with an owner and often a date. The executive accountable for it reads every update on their next pass, so an update has to be true and specific.

1. `my_promises` returns what this seat owes, and separately what its area answers for. Keep the two apart: the seat may update only what is written in its own name.
2. For each promise the seat owes, establish what actually happened. Read the related tasks and deliverables, and ask the person. Do not infer progress from silence.
3. Propose one update for each: a state (`on_track`, `at_risk`, `blocked` or `completed`) and a note saying what was done, what is in the way or what is needed and from whom. A state with no note records nothing, and a note that only repeats the state is no better.
4. **Write the updates with `update_promise` only after the person agrees them**, one at a time. Mark a promise `completed` only when its done line is met and the evidence is filed.
5. For a promise the area answers for but somebody else owes, do not update it. Tell the person who owes it and what looks stuck, and offer Hand Over if work should move.

Do not invent a due date, an owner or progress nobody reported. A promise that has nothing happening against it, and no date, is a finding worth stating plainly.

**PT:** “Atualize minhas promessas da semana.” Propose each state with a real note, then write only what is approved. **EN:** “Is anything I owe at risk?” Read, evidence, recommend, and write nothing yet.
