# What Needs Me

Read [the seat protocol](references/seat-protocol.md) before proceeding, then follow the seat's own procedures from `how_this_seat_works`; they override this skill on how the work is done.

Most of what looks like a stalled team is one unanswered question. This skill makes everything waiting visible and helps the person clear it. It never answers for them.

1. `what_is_waiting_for_me` returns the decisions nobody has answered, the alerts workers raised, the promises the person owes and the work that gave up and escalated. If the seat is not permitted to read this, the device says so; report that and stop.
2. Group the items by what each needs: a judgement, information, a signature, or nothing at all (stale alerts). Lead with what unblocks the most work. Read the tasks or deliverables behind an item before summarising it.
3. For each decision, **draft** the answer the person might give: the options as the record states them, a recommendation and the reason. The person answers decisions on the device. No seat can, and this one does not try.
4. When the person says to stop something and do something else, that is `change_direction`. It is one act: the reason goes into the organisation's decision log, the promises named are superseded, running work for them is cancelled, and the new work is filed with its done line. Show them the decision, the reason (at least a sentence; it is what anyone will read in six months), the promise numbers it replaces and the new work, and **call it only on their explicit word**. It changes what is being done about a goal. It never changes the goal.
5. For a promise the person owes, write what they report doing with Keep Promises, and only with their words and their agreement.

Never set an objective, answer a decision, approve work or employ a worker. Never mark something handled because it is old.

**PT:** “O que está esperando por mim?” Group, recommend, and draft answers without answering. **EN:** “Drop the newsletter, put the time into the course launch.” Prepare one change of direction and call it on their word.
